\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
x^{4}+xy+4y^{3}+y+1=0
\end{equation}
has no integer solutions.
\end{theorem}

Replacing $y$ by $-y$ transforms \eqref{eq:main} into $f(x,y)=0$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f}
f(x,y)=4y^3+xy+y-x^4-1,
\end{equation}
that is, $4y^3+xy+y=x^4+1$. We prove that $f(x,y)=0$ has no integer solutions; all statements below refer to the coordinates of \eqref{eq:f}.

The proof combines two reciprocity arguments. In Section~\ref{sec:cubic}, a graph of cubic Hilbert symbols over $\Q(\omega)$, with $76$ linear auxiliary polynomials and $542$ edges, shows that every integer solution satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mod7}
(x,y)\bmod 7\in\{(0,4),(0,5),(2,2),(4,6)\}.
\end{equation}
In Section~\ref{sec:octic}, a graph of Hilbert symbols of order $8$ over $\Q(\zeta_8)$, with $77$ linear auxiliary polynomials and $519$ edges, defines an element $\mathcal A$ of the Brauer group of the curve $f=0$. At an integer solution the local invariants of $\mathcal A$ would have to add up to $0$; in Section~\ref{sec:proof} we show that they cannot on the residue classes \eqref{eq:mod7}.

Section~\ref{sec:cubic} uses only the explicit formula for tame Hilbert symbols and the Hilbert reciprocity law. Section~\ref{sec:octic} also quotes standard facts on Brauer groups of curves: residues and their compatibility with corestriction, purity, and the description of the local evaluation maps by the Tate pairing. Apart from these, the proof uses polynomial identities modulo the restrictions of $f$ to the auxiliary lines and finite computations with residues; all their data are listed in Section~\ref{sec:data}. The whole proof is also formalized in Lean~4 in the file \texttt{Solution.lean}, see Section~\ref{sec:lean}.

\section{Hilbert symbols of order 3 and 8}\label{sec:symbols}

\paragraph{The fields.} Let $\omega$ be a primitive cube root of unity and $\zeta$ a primitive $8$th root of unity, so that $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$ and $\zeta^4=-1$. For $n=3$ we put $K_3=\Q(\omega)$ and $\zeta_3=\omega$, and for $n=8$ we put $K_8=\Q(\zeta)$ and $\zeta_8=\zeta$. Both fields are totally complex; their rings of integers are $\Z[\omega]$ and $\Z[\zeta]$. In $K_3$ we write $\langle A,B\rangle=A+B\omega$, also when $A$ and $B$ are polynomials in $x$ and $y$; then $\langle A,B\rangle\langle C,D\rangle=\langle AC-BD,\ AD+BC-BD\rangle$, and the norm is $N(\langle A,B\rangle)=A^2-AB+B^2$. The Galois group of $K_8/\Q$ is $G=\{\sigma_1,\sigma_3,\sigma_5,\sigma_7\}$, where $\sigma_c(\zeta)=\zeta^c$; we identify $G$ with $(\Z/8\Z)^\times$, and note that $c^2\equiv1\pmod8$ for every $c\in G$.

For a finite place $v$ of $K_n$ we write $K_{n,v}$ for the completion, $v(a)$ for the normalized valuation, and call $a$ a unit at $v$ if $v(a)=0$. In $K_3$, a prime $p\equiv2\pmod3$ is inert, with residue field $\Z[\omega]/p\Z[\omega]$ with $p^2$ elements. If $p\equiv1\pmod3$, the polynomial $t^2+t+1$ has two roots $r$ modulo $p$, and for each of them there is a place $v_r$ above $p$, at which $\omega$ is the root of $t^2+t+1$ in $\Z_p$ congruent to $r$ modulo $p$; its residue field is $\F_p$, and $\omega$ reduces to $r$. The prime $3$ is ramified: $\pi=1-\omega$ generates the prime ideal above $3$, $\pi^2=-3\omega$, and $\Z[\omega]/\pi\Z[\omega]=\F_3$. In $K_8$, an odd prime $p$ has four places above it with residue field $\F_p$ if $p\equiv1\pmod8$, and two places with residue field $\F_{p^2}$ otherwise; $2$ is totally ramified, with uniformizer $\lambda=1-\zeta$.

\paragraph{Hilbert symbols.} For a place $v$ of $K_n$ and $a,b\in K_{n,v}^\times$, the Hilbert symbol $(a,b)_v$ of order $n$ is an $n$th root of unity \cite[Chapter~V, \S3]{N}. We write $(a,b)_v=\zeta_n^{\beta_v(a,b)}$ with $\beta_v(a,b)\in\Z/n\Z$, and we use the following facts \cite[Chapter~V, \S3 and Chapter~VI, \S8]{N}.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(H1)] $\beta_v(a,bc)=\beta_v(a,b)+\beta_v(a,c)$, $\beta_v(a,b)=-\beta_v(b,a)$, and $\beta_v(a,c^n)=0$.
\item[(H2)] Let $v$ be a finite place not above $n$, let $q$ be the number of elements of its residue field, and let $\varpi$ be a uniformizer at $v$. Write $a=\varpi^eu$ and $b=\varpi^fw$ with units $u$ and $w$, and define $\chi_v(u)\in\Z/n\Z$ by $\bar u^{(q-1)/n}=\bar\zeta_n^{\,\chi_v(u)}$, where the bar denotes the reduction to the residue field. Then $\beta_v(a,b)=e\,\chi_v(w)-f\,\chi_v(u)+ef\,\chi_v(-1)$. In particular, $\beta_v(a,b)=0$ if $a$ and $b$ are both units.
\item[(H3)] (Hilbert reciprocity.) For $a,b\in K_n^\times$, $\beta_v(a,b)=0$ for all but finitely many places $v$, and $\sum_v\beta_v(a,b)=0$, where the sum is over all places of $K_n$. At the complex places, $\beta_v(a,b)=0$.
\end{itemize}
For $n=3$, $\chi_v(-1)=0$ because $-1=(-1)^3$. Formula (H2) fixes the normalization of the symbols; with the opposite sign convention all values $\beta_v$ change sign, and the proof below is the same. For $n=8$ we also use the behaviour under $G$: the symbol is compatible with field isomorphisms, $(\sigma a,\sigma b)_{\sigma v}=\sigma\bigl((a,b)_v\bigr)$, and $\sigma_c(\zeta^\beta)=\zeta^{c\beta}$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:galois}
\beta_{\sigma_c v}(\sigma_c a,\sigma_c b)=c\,\beta_v(a,b).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Classes in $K_3$ at the places not above $3$.} Let $p\neq3$ be a prime and $v$ a place of $K_3$ above $p$. For $a=p^eu\in K_{3,v}^\times$ with a unit $u$, put $c_v(a)=(e\bmod3,\ \chi_v(u))\in\F_3^2$. By (H1) and (H2), $c_v$ is additive, $c_v(a)$ determines the class of $a$ modulo cubes, and $\beta_v(a,b)=e\chi_v(w)-f\chi_v(u)$ for $c_v(a)=(e,\chi_v(u))$ and $c_v(b)=(f,\chi_v(w))$. We put $c_p(a)=c_v(a)\in\F_3^2$ if $p\equiv2\pmod3$, and $c_p(a)=(c_{v_r}(a),c_{v_{r'}}(a))\in\F_3^4$ if $p\equiv1\pmod3$, where $r<r'$ are the roots of $t^2+t+1$ in $\{1,\dots,p-1\}$; accordingly, $\beta_p$ is the sum of the forms $\beta_v$ over the places $v$ above $p$. For $p\equiv2\pmod3$ and a rational integer $u$ prime to $p$, we have $\bar u\in\F_p$, so that $\bar u^{(p^2-1)/3}=(\bar u^{p-1})^{(p+1)/3}=1$ and $\chi_v(u)=0$.

\paragraph{The place above 3 in $K_3$.}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:M3}
Let $v$ be the place of $K_3$ above $3$. Every non-zero element of $K_{3,v}$ is equal to $\pi^a\omega^b2^c(1+3\omega)^d$ times a cube, for unique $a,b,c,d\in\Z/3\Z$, and every unit congruent to $1$ modulo $9$ is a cube. Put $c_3(A)=(a,b,c,d)\in\F_3^4$. Then $c_3$ is additive, and $\beta_v(A,B)=c_3(A)^{\mathsf T}M_3\,c_3(B)$, where
\[
M_3=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&2&0\\0&0&1&2\\1&2&0&0\\0&1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\pmod3.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\alpha\in\Z[\omega]$, the polynomial $g(s)=s+3s^2+3s^3-\alpha$ satisfies $g'(s)\equiv1\pmod\pi$, so by Hensel's lemma it has a root $s$ in the completion of $\Z[\omega]$, and then $(1+3s)^3=1+9(s+3s^2+3s^3)=1+9\alpha$. Hence every unit congruent to $1$ modulo $9$ is a cube in $K_{3,v}$, and the class of a unit modulo cubes depends only on its residue modulo $9$. There are $54$ units in $\Z[\omega]/(9)$, and a direct enumeration shows that the $27$ products $\omega^b2^c(1+3\omega)^d$ with $b,c,d\in\{0,1,2\}$ are pairwise distinct modulo $9$, also after multiplication by $-1=(-1)^3$. Hence they represent all $27$ classes of units modulo cubes. Since $\pi^3$ is a cube times a unit, the exponent of $\pi$ is the valuation modulo $3$, and the first statement follows.

By (H1), it remains to compute $\beta_v$ on the $16$ ordered pairs of the elements $\pi$, $\omega$, $2$ and $1+3\omega$. Their norms are $3$, $1$, $4$ and $7$, hence, for each such pair, all the symbols at the places not above $2$, $3$ and $7$ vanish by (H2), and, by (H3), $\beta_v$ at the place above $3$ is minus the sum of the values at the place above $2$ and at the two places above $7$. These places correspond to $\bar\omega=2$ and $\bar\omega=4$ in $\F_7$. The classes $(e,\chi)$ of the four elements at these places are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 & 2 & \bar\omega=2 & \bar\omega=4\\\hline
\pi & (0,2) & (0,0) & (0,2)\\
\omega & (0,1) & (0,2) & (0,2)\\
2 & (1,0) & (0,2) & (0,1)\\
1+3\omega & (0,2) & (1,0) & (0,0)
\end{array}
\]
where, at the places above $7$, $\chi(u)$ is defined by $\bar u^2=\bar\omega^{\chi(u)}$ in $\F_7$, and $7$ is a uniformizer. Formula (H2) and the sum over these three places give $M_3$.
\end{proof}

We write $\beta_3$ for the form of Lemma~\ref{lem:M3}. Since $3=-\omega^2\pi^2$ and $4=2^2$, we have $c_3(3)=(2,2,0,0)$ and $c_3(4)=(0,0,2,0)$.

\section{The cubic graph}\label{sec:cubic}

\paragraph{The data.} The cubic graph consists of $76$ polynomials $H_i=\langle P_i,Q_i\rangle$, $0\leq i\leq75$, where $P_i,Q_i\in\Z[x,y]$ have degree at most $1$, of constants $c_i\in\Z[\omega]$, and of $542$ edges $(i,j)$ with $i<j$ and weights $e_{ij}\in\{1,2\}$. They are listed in Section~\ref{sec:data-cubic}. The vertices $0,\dots,5$ are \emph{real}: $Q_i=0$. The other $70$ vertices form $35$ pairs of complex conjugate vertices $i,\bar\imath$, with $H_{\bar\imath}=\bar H_i$, and the graph is invariant under complex conjugation: with an edge $(i,j)$ of weight $e$ it contains the edge $(\bar\imath,\bar\jmath)$ of weight $-e$, after reordering its endpoints (an edge between $i$ and $\bar\imath$ is its own conjugate).

Let $(x,y)$ be an integer solution of $f(x,y)=0$ at which all the values $H_i=H_i(x,y)$ are non-zero. For a place $v$ of $K_3$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bv3}
B_v=\sum_{(i,j)}e_{ij}\,\beta_v(H_i,H_j)+\sum_i\beta_v(H_i,c_i)\in\Z/3\Z,
\end{equation}
the first sum over the edges, and $B_p=\sum_{v\mid p}B_v$ for every prime $p$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rec3}
At such a solution, $\sum_pB_p=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply (H3) to every pair $(H_i,H_j)$ of an edge and to every pair $(H_i,c_i)$, multiply by the weights and add; the complex place contributes nothing.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nonzero3}
No $H_i$ vanishes at an integer solution of $f(x,y)=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For integers $x,y$, $H_i(x,y)=0$ if and only if $P_i(x,y)=Q_i(x,y)=0$, since $1$ and $\omega$ are linearly independent over $\Q$. For a real vertex this is a line defined over $\Q$, and for the other vertices it is a point with rational coordinates. A direct computation shows that the only rational point of $f=0$ on these lines and points is $(0,\tfrac12)$, which lies on $H_0,H_5,H_{34},H_{35},H_{36},H_{37}$, and it is not integral.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Good primes.} Let $i$ be a vertex, and write $H_i=\alpha_ix+\beta_iy+\gamma_i$ with $\alpha_i,\beta_i,\gamma_i\in\Z[\omega]$. If $\beta_i\neq0$, we parametrize the line $H_i=0$ by $t=x$, $y=-(\alpha_it+\gamma_i)/\beta_i$, put $a_i=\beta_i$ and $f_i(t)=\beta_i^3f\bigl(t,y(t)\bigr)$; otherwise by $x=-\gamma_i/\alpha_i$, $t=y$, and put $a_i=\alpha_i$ and $f_i(t)=\alpha_i^4f\bigl(x,t\bigr)$. Then $f_i\in\Z[\omega][t]$, with leading coefficient $-\beta_i^3$, respectively $4\alpha_i^4$. Let $g_{ij}(t)\in K_3[t]$ be the restriction of $H_j$ to the line, so that $a_ig_{ij}\in\Z[\omega][t]$. Put $\rho_{ij}=e_{ij}$ and $\rho_{ji}=3-e_{ij}$ for an edge $(i,j)$, $i<j$, so that $\rho_{ij}\in\{1,2\}$, $\rho_{ij}=0$ if $i$ and $j$ are not joined, and
\[
A_i(t)=c_i\prod_jg_{ij}(t)^{\rho_{ij}}.
\]
We use two kinds of identities. For every edge $(i,j)$ with $i<j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sep3}
\alpha_{ij}(t)f_i(t)+\gamma_{ij}(t)\,a_ig_{ij}(t)=D_{ij}
\end{equation}
with polynomials $\alpha_{ij},\gamma_{ij}$ over $\Z[\omega]$ and a non-zero constant $D_{ij}\in\Z[\omega]$; we take $D_{ij}=\operatorname{Res}_t(f_i,a_ig_{ij})$. For every vertex $i$, with an integer $D_i\geq1$ and a polynomial $w_i\in\Z[\omega][t]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertex3}
D_i^3A_i(t)\equiv w_i(t)^3\pmod{f_i(t)}\quad\text{in }K_3[t].
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:good3}
Suppose that \eqref{eq:sep3} holds for every edge and \eqref{eq:vertex3} for every vertex. Let $S$ be a finite set of primes containing $2$ and $3$ and the primes dividing the norms of the $c_i$, of the $a_i$, of the $D_{ij}$, and the $D_i$. Then $B_p=0$ for every prime $p\notin S$, at every integer solution.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $v$ be a place above $p\notin S$. By (H2), every term of \eqref{eq:Bv3} in which both arguments are units at $v$ vanishes. Let $i$ be a vertex with $v(H_i)>0$, and let $t$ be the value of the parameter at the solution. Since $a_i$ is a unit at $v$, the other coordinate given by the parametrization is congruent modulo $v$ to that of the solution; hence $f_i(t)\equiv0$ and $g_{ij}(t)\equiv H_j$ modulo $v$, and all the coefficients involved are $v$-integral. If $v$ also divides $H_j$ for an edge $(i,j)$, \eqref{eq:sep3} (for the smaller of $i,j$) gives $v\mid D_{ij}$, which is impossible. Hence every edge with a non-zero term has exactly one endpoint divisible by $v$, and by (H1) all terms involving a vertex $i$ with $v\mid H_i$ combine to $\beta_v(H_i,c_i\prod_jH_j^{\rho_{ij}})$, where we used $\rho_{ji}\equiv-e_{ij}\pmod3$. The quotient of $D_i^3A_i-w_i^3$ by $f_i$ has coefficients whose denominators divide powers of $2$ and of $a_i$, because the leading coefficient of $f_i$ is $-a_i^3$ or $4a_i^4$; so \eqref{eq:vertex3} gives $D_i^3c_i\prod_jH_j^{\rho_{ij}}\equiv w_i(t)^3$ modulo $v$, and the left side is a unit at $v$. Hence $c_i\prod_jH_j^{\rho_{ij}}$ is a non-zero cube in the residue field, and the symbol vanishes by (H2). Hence $B_v=0$, and $B_p=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:checks3}
The hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:good3} hold with
\[
S=\{2,3,5,7,13,19,37\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The norms of the constants $c_i$ are $\{2,3,7,19\}$-units, and the norms of the $a_i$ are $\{2,3\}$-units. For every edge, Table~\ref{tab:res3} gives the factorization of the norm of $D_{ij}$; all are supported on $\{2,3,5,7,13,19\}$. For every vertex, Table~\ref{tab:id3} gives $D_i$, which is a $\{2,3,37\}$-unit, and $w_i$; then \eqref{eq:vertex3} holds, as division by $f_i$ shows.
\end{proof}

The graph is the closure under complex conjugation of a graph with $276$ edges in which the vertex identities only hold in a relaxed form, $A_i\equiv\bar A_{\bar\imath}W_i^3$; taking the closure turns them into the identities \eqref{eq:vertex3} and multiplies all local values by $2$.

\paragraph{Residue classes.} The local computations use the following description of the classes of the values of a polynomial on a residue class.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:residue}
Let $p$ be a prime, $k\geq1$, and let $z\in\Z[\omega]$ be non-zero with $z\equiv\zeta\pmod{p^k}$, where $\zeta=\langle A,B\rangle$ with $0\leq A,B<p^k$. Then $c_p(z)\in b+D$ for the vector $b$ and the subspace $D$ given as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $p\equiv2\pmod3$. If $\zeta\neq0$, then $b=c_p(\zeta)$ and $D=0$. If $\zeta=0$, then $b=0$, and $D$ is $\F_3^2$, or the span of $(1,0)$ if $z\in\Z$.
\item[(ii)] $p\equiv1\pmod3$. For each root $r$, let $\rho$ be the root of $t^2+t+1$ modulo $p^k$ congruent to $r$. If $A+B\rho\not\equiv0\pmod{p^k}$, the two coordinates of $c_{v_r}(z)$ are those of $c_{v_r}(\zeta)$; otherwise these two coordinates are arbitrary.
\item[(iii)] $p=3$. If $\zeta\neq0$, let $a=v(\zeta)<2k$ be its valuation at $\pi$ and $m=2k-a$. Then $b=c_3(\zeta)$, and $D=0$ if $m\geq4$, while $D=D_m$ if $m\leq3$, where $D_1$ is spanned by $(0,1,0,0)$, $(0,0,1,0)$ and $(0,0,0,1)$, $D_2$ by $(0,0,1,0)$ and $(0,0,0,1)$, and $D_3$ by $(0,0,1,1)$. If $\zeta=0$, then $b=0$ and $D=\F_3^4$.
\item[(iv)] $p=3$, $z\in\Z$, so that $B=0$. If $3^k\nmid A$, let $j=v_3(A)$. Then $b=c_3(A)$ and $D=0$ if $k-j\geq2$, while $b=j\,c_3(3)$ and $D$ is spanned by $c_3(4)$ if $k-j=1$. If $3^k\mid A$, then $b=0$ and $D$ is spanned by $c_3(3)$ and $c_3(4)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) If $\zeta\neq0$, then $e=v(z)=v(\zeta)<k$ and $z/p^e\equiv\zeta/p^e\pmod p$, so that $\chi$ is determined. If $z\in\Z$, its unit part is a rational integer, and $\chi=0$. (ii) At $v_r$, $z$ and $\zeta$ are the $p$-adic numbers $A'+B'\omega$ and $A+B\omega$ with $\omega\equiv\rho\pmod{p^k}$, and the argument of (i) applies. (iii) If $\zeta\neq0$, then $v(z)=a$, and $z/\pi^a\equiv\zeta/\pi^a\pmod{\pi^m}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:M3}, the class of a unit depends only on its residue modulo $9=\omega\pi^4$; the units congruent to $1$ modulo $\pi^m$ form a subgroup, and a direct enumeration of their residues modulo $9$ shows that their classes form $D_m$. (iv) If $3^k\nmid A$, then $z=3^ju$ with a rational unit $u\equiv A/3^j\pmod{3^{k-j}}$. The class of a rational unit depends only on its residue modulo $9$, the units $\pm1$ are cubes, and the rational units congruent to $1$ modulo $3$ are $4^s$ times a unit congruent to $1$ modulo $9$; hence the classes are as stated, and if $3^k\mid A$ the valuation $j$ is also unknown.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:envelope}
Let $p$ be a prime, and consider vertices $i$ with vectors $b_i$, subspaces $D_i$ and vectors $\gamma_i$, and a set of edges $(i,j)$ with $i<j$ and weights $e_{ij}\in\{1,2\}$. With $\rho_{ij}$ as above, put $\sigma_i=\gamma_i+\sum_j\rho_{ij}b_j$. Suppose that $\beta_p(d,\sigma_i)=0$ for every $i$ and every $d\in D_i$, and that $\beta_p(d,d')=0$ for every edge $(i,j)$ and all $d\in D_i$, $d'\in D_j$. Then
\[
\sum_{(i,j)}e_{ij}\beta_p(c_i,c_j)+\sum_i\beta_p(c_i,\gamma_i)=\sum_{(i,j)}e_{ij}\beta_p(b_i,b_j)+\sum_i\beta_p(b_i,\gamma_i)
\]
whenever $c_i\in b_i+D_i$ for every $i$. By bilinearity, it suffices to check the hypotheses for $d$ and $d'$ in spanning sets of $D_i$ and $D_j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $c_i=b_i+d_i$ with $d_i\in D_i$. As $\beta_p$ is bilinear and $\beta_p(u,v)=-\beta_p(v,u)$, the left side equals the right side plus $\sum_i\beta_p(d_i,\sigma_i)+\sum_{(i,j)}e_{ij}\beta_p(d_i,d_j)$, and both sums vanish.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Local values.} Let $p\in S$. Here $2$ and $5$ are inert, the places above $7$ are $v_2$ and $v_4$, those above $13$ are $v_3$ and $v_9$, those above $19$ are $v_7$ and $v_{11}$, and those above $37$ are $v_{10}$ and $v_{26}$. Suppose that $x\equiv a$ and $y\equiv b\pmod{p^k}$. Then $H_i(x,y)\equiv H_i(a,b)\pmod{p^k}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:residue}, applied with $\zeta$ the residue of $H_i(a,b)$, gives a vector $b_i$ and a subspace $D_i$ with $c_p(H_i)\in b_i+D_i$; for the real vertices, $H_i\in\Z$, and we use this in (i) and (iv). With $\gamma_i=c_p(c_i)$, if the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:envelope} hold, then $B_p$ is determined by $a$, $b$ and $k$. Starting from the solutions of $f\equiv0\pmod p$, we refine a residue class $x\equiv a$, $y\equiv b\pmod{p^k}$ to the classes modulo $p^{k+1}$ that contain solutions of $f\equiv0\pmod{p^{k+1}}$, until these hypotheses hold, or until no solution remains. Table~\ref{tab:local3} lists the resulting $1068$ terminal classes and the value of $B_p$ in each; every point of $U(\Z_p)$, where $U$ is the curve $f=0$, lies in one of them.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local3}
At every integer solution of $f(x,y)=0$,
\[
B_2=1,\quad B_3=2,\quad B_5=0,\quad B_{13}=0,\quad B_{19}=2,\quad B_{37}=0,
\]
and $B_7=1$ if $(x,y)\bmod7$ is one of $(0,4),(0,5),(2,2),(4,6)$, while $B_7=2$ if it is one of $(1,3),(3,2),(5,1)$. These seven residue classes are all the solutions of $f\equiv0\pmod7$, and all of them are smooth points modulo $7$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is read off from Table~\ref{tab:local3}: for $p\neq7$ all the classes have the value stated, and for $p=7$ the value in each class is the one stated for its reduction modulo $7$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mod7}
Every integer solution of $f(x,y)=0$ satisfies \eqref{eq:mod7}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:nonzero3}, the sums $B_p$ are defined, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:rec3}, Proposition~\ref{prop:good3} and Lemma~\ref{lem:checks3}, $B_2+B_3+B_5+B_7+B_{13}+B_{19}+B_{37}=0$ in $\Z/3\Z$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:local3} this reads $1+2+0+B_7+0+2+0=0$, that is, $B_7=1$.
\end{proof}

\section{The graph of order 8}\label{sec:octic}

\paragraph{The curve.} Let $U$ be the affine curve $f(x,y)=0$ over $\Q$, and $X$ its closure in $\mathbb P^2$. The curve $X$ is a smooth plane quartic, of genus $3$, and its only point at infinity is the rational point $\infty=[0:1:0]$. Write $K=K_8=\Q(\zeta)$. The curve $U$ contains the rational point $Q_0=(0,\tfrac12)$ and the four points $R_k=(\zeta^k,0)$, $k\in\{1,3,5,7\}$, which form one closed point of $U$ with residue field $K$. We call these five points the \emph{special points}.

\paragraph{Brauer classes.} For a field $F$ of characteristic $0$ containing $\zeta$ and $a,b\in F^\times$ let $(a,b)_8\in\Br(F)$ be the class of the cyclic algebra defined by $\zeta$, $a$ and $b$; it is bilinear, $(a,b)_8=-(b,a)_8$, and $8(a,b)_8=0$. For a completion of a number field containing $\zeta$, $\inv(a,b)_8=\beta(a,b)/8$ with the $\beta$ of Section~\ref{sec:symbols} \cite[Chapter~V, \S3]{N}. Applying $\sigma_c\in G$ to the coefficients maps $(a,b)_8$ to $c\,(\sigma_ca,\sigma_cb)_8$, by the same argument as \eqref{eq:galois}. We use the following standard facts on the Brauer group of the smooth projective curve $X$ \cite{P}:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(B1)] Every class $\alpha\in\Br(\Q(X))$ has a residue $\partial_x\alpha\in H^1(\Q(x),\Q/\Z)$ at every closed point $x$ of $X$; $\alpha\in\Br(X)$ if and only if all these residues vanish (purity), and then $\alpha$ can be evaluated at every point of $X$. For a finite extension $L/\Q$, residues commute with corestriction: $\partial_x\Cor_{L(X)/\Q(X)}\beta=\sum_{y\mapsto x}\Cor_{L(y)/\Q(x)}\partial_y\beta$.
\item[(B2)] The corestrictions to $\Q$ of the residues of $\alpha$ at all closed points add up to $0$.
\item[(B3)] For a regular proper model $\mathcal X$ over $\Z_p$, a class in $\Br(X\otimes\Q_p)$ whose residue along the special fibre vanishes extends to $\Br(\mathcal X)$, and then $\inv_p\alpha(P)=0$ for all $P\in X(\Q_p)$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The data.} Let $g_1,\dots,g_{25}\in\Z[\zeta]$ be the elements listed in Section~\ref{sec:data-octic}: $g_1=\zeta$ and $g_2=1+\zeta+\zeta^2$ are units, $g_3=1+\zeta$ lies above $2$, and the others are generators of prime ideals above $3,5,7,11,13,17,41,73$. Put
\[
S=\{2,3,5,7,11,13,17,41,73\}.
\]
The graph of order $8$ consists of $77$ lines $H_i=\alpha_ix+\beta_iy+\gamma_i$ with $\alpha_i,\beta_i,\gamma_i\in\Z[\zeta]$, of constants $c_i$, which are products of the $g_k$, and of $519$ edges $(i,j)$ with $i<j$ and weights $e_{ij}\in\{1,\dots,7\}$; see Section~\ref{sec:data-octic}. Put
\[
B=\sum_{(i,j)}e_{ij}(H_i,H_j)_8+\sum_i(H_i,c_i)_8\in\Br(K(X)),\qquad \mathcal A=\Cor_{K(X)/\Q(X)}B\in\Br(\Q(X)).
\]
For a prime $p$ and a point $P\in U(\Q_p)$ at which all $H_i(P)$ are non-zero, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bp}
b_p(P)=\sum_{v\mid p}\Bigl(\sum_{(i,j)}e_{ij}\beta_v\bigl(H_i(P),H_j(P)\bigr)+\sum_i\beta_v\bigl(H_i(P),c_i\bigr)\Bigr)\in\Z/8\Z,
\end{equation}
the sum over the places $v$ of $K$ above $p$. Then $\inv_p\mathcal A(P)=b_p(P)/8$, because the invariant of a corestriction is the sum of the invariants.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nonzero8}
No $H_i$ vanishes at an integer solution of $f(x,y)=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For integers $x,y$, $H_i(x,y)=0$ means four linear equations with integer coefficients, the coefficients of $1,\zeta,\zeta^2,\zeta^3$. For $56$ lines they have no rational solution. For $15$ lines their only rational solution is one point, which is $(0,\tfrac12)$ or one of $(1,1)$, $(0,0)$, $(-1,-1)$, $(-1,1)$, $(1,0)$, $(-1,0)$, where $f$ takes the non-zero values $4,-1,-6,2,-2,-2$. The remaining six lines are defined over $\Q$: $H_{26}=y$, $H_{27}=2y-1$, $H_{30}=x+1$, $H_{40}=x$, $H_{49}=x+2y-1$ and $H_{70}=x-1$. On them $f$ restricts to $-x^4-1$, to a function of the non-integer $y=\frac12$, to $4y^3-2$, to $(2y-1)(2y^2+y+1)$, to $-2(2y-1)(4y^3-7y^2+3y-1)$ with $x=1-2y$, and to $2(2y^3+y-1)$, none of which has an integer zero.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The Galois closure.} The $77$ lines have $152$ distinct Galois conjugates $\sigma_c(H_i)$, where $\sigma_c$ acts on the coefficients, and they form $49$ orbits under $G$. We regard $B$ as a graph on these $152$ lines, the $75$ lines not listed carrying neither edges nor constants. By the rule $(a,b)_8\mapsto c\,(\sigma_ca,\sigma_cb)_8$ and $\Cor\circ\sigma_c^*=\Cor$, moving an edge or a constant from a line to a conjugate line, with the weight multiplied by $c$, does not change $\mathcal A$; the graph listed in Section~\ref{sec:data-octic} was obtained in this way from a graph whose edges are placed at representatives of the orbits.

For a line $\ell$ of the closure put $A_\ell=c_\ell\prod_jH_j^{\rho_{\ell j}}$, where $\rho_{ij}=e_{ij}$ and $\rho_{ji}=-e_{ij}$ for an edge $(i,j)$, and $c_\ell=1$, $A_\ell=1$ for the lines not listed; $A_\ell$ is a function on $\ell=0$. Let $\Gamma_\ell\subseteq G$ be the stabilizer of $\ell$, and put
\[
\tilde A_\ell=\prod_{c}\sigma_c\bigl(A_{\sigma_c\ell}\bigr)^{c},\qquad N_\Gamma(A)=\prod_{m\in\Gamma}\sigma_m(A)^m,
\]
the first product over representatives $c$ of $G/\Gamma_\ell$, where $\sigma_c\ell$ is the line $\sigma_c(\ell)$ and $\sigma_c$ acts on the coefficients; $\tilde A_\ell$ is a function on the line $\ell=0$, and $N_{\Gamma_\ell}(\tilde A_\ell)=\prod_{c\in G}\sigma_c(A_{\sigma_c\ell})^c$ does not change under the moves just described. For a field $F\supseteq K$ let $\kappa\colon F^\times\to H^1(F,\Z/8\Z)$ be the Kummer map with respect to $\zeta$. The edges of $B$ join lines which meet at an \emph{anchor}, a point $P\in K^2$ at which $f(P)$ is a non-zero $S$-unit; in particular two lines joined by an edge never meet on $X$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:res8}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] Let $x$ be a closed point of $U$, not a special point, lying on the line $\ell$ and on no line of another $G$-orbit, and suppose that no point of $X_K$ above $x$ lies on two different lines of the orbit of $\ell$. Let $y$ be a point of $X_K$ above $x$ on $\ell=0$, $\mu$ the intersection multiplicity of $\ell$ and $X$ at $y$, and $D\subseteq\Gamma_\ell$ the image of $\operatorname{Gal}(K(y)/\Q(x))$ in $G$. Then $\partial_x\mathcal A=\mu\Cor_{K(y)/\Q(x)}\kappa(Z_y)$ with $Z_y=\prod_\gamma\sigma_\gamma(\tilde A_\ell)(y)^\gamma$, the product over representatives $\gamma$ of $\Gamma_\ell/D$, and its restriction to $K(y)$ is $\mu\kappa\bigl(N_{\Gamma_\ell}(\tilde A_\ell)(y)\bigr)$. If $N_{\Gamma_\ell}(\tilde A_\ell)(y)$ is an $8$th power in $K(y)$, then $\partial_x\mathcal A$ is a homomorphism $D\to4\Z/8\Z$, whose value at the Frobenius of a prime $\mathfrak p\nmid2$ of $\Q(x)$ of degree one, unramified in $K(y)$, at which $Z_y$ is a unit, is $\mu\sum_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}\chi_{\mathfrak P}(Z_y)$.
\item[(ii)] At the closed point $\{R_k\}$, $\partial\mathcal A=\kappa(Z_R)$, where we identify its residue field with $K$ by $R_1$ and
\[
Z_R=\prod_{m\in G}\ \prod_{\ell\ni R_m}\sigma_m\bigl(A_\ell(R_m)\bigr)^{m\mu_\ell(R_m)},
\]
the inner product over the lines $\ell$ of the closure through $R_m$, with the intersection multiplicity $\mu_\ell(R_m)$ of $\ell$ and $X$ at $R_m$. At $Q_0$, $\partial\mathcal A=\Cor_{K/\Q}\kappa(Z_Q)$ with $Z_Q=\prod_{\ell\ni Q_0}A_\ell(Q_0)^{\mu_\ell(Q_0)}$; it vanishes if and only if $N_G(Z_Q)$ is an $8$th power in $K$ and $\sum_{w\mid q}\chi_w(Z_Q)=0$ for one prime $q\equiv3$ and one prime $q\equiv5\pmod8$ at which $Z_Q$ is a unit.
\end{itemize}
If $x$ lies on lines of several orbits, its residue is the sum of the contributions of the orbits, since lines through a common point of $X$ are never joined by an edge.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By (B1), $\partial_x\mathcal A=\sum_{y'}\Cor_{K(y')/\Q(x)}\partial_{y'}B$, the sum over the points $y'$ of $X_K$ above $x$, and $\partial_{y'}B=\mu\kappa(A_j(y'))$ if $y'$ lies on the line $j$ only, because the lines joined to $j$ by edges do not vanish at $y'$. The points above $x$ are the $\sigma_c(y)$, $c\in G/D$, and $\sigma_c(y)$ lies only on the line $\sigma_c(\ell)$ of the orbit; in particular $D\subseteq\Gamma_\ell$. The automorphism $\sigma_c$ of $X_K$ over $X$ maps $y$ to $\sigma_c(y)$ and acts on $K$ by $\sigma_c$; since the Kummer map for $\zeta^c$ is $c^{-1}$ times the Kummer map for $\zeta$ and $c^{-1}\equiv c\pmod8$, transporting the residue at $\sigma_c(y)$ to $y$ gives $\mu\kappa\bigl(\sigma_c(A_{\sigma_c\ell})(y)^c\bigr)$, without changing the corestriction. Writing $c=c'\gamma$ with $c'$ in $G/\Gamma_\ell$ and $\gamma$ in $\Gamma_\ell/D$ and using $\sigma_{c'\gamma}(\ell)=\sigma_{c'}(\ell)$, the sum of these terms is $\mu\kappa(Z_y)$. The restriction of $\Cor_{K(y)/\Q(x)}$ to $K(y)$ is $\sum_{\delta\in D}\delta^*$, and $\delta$ fixes the coordinates of $y$, which lie in $\Q(x)$; so the restriction is $\mu\kappa(N_{\Gamma_\ell}(\tilde A_\ell)(y))$. If this vanishes, the residue lies in the kernel of the restriction, which is $\operatorname{Hom}(D,\Z/8\Z)$, of exponent $2$ since $D\subseteq(\Z/8\Z)^\times$; the value of a corestriction at $\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak p}$ is the sum of the values at the $\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak P}$, $\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p$ \cite{N}. Part (ii) is the same computation with all lines through the point: above $\{R_k\}$ lie the four points $R_m$, with residue field $K$, and the residue at $R_m$, $\kappa\bigl(\prod_{\ell\ni R_m}A_\ell(R_m)^{\mu_\ell(R_m)}\bigr)$, is transported to $R_1=\sigma_m(R_m)$ by $\sigma_m$; above $Q_0$ lies the single point $Q_0$, with residue field $K$. Finally, $\Cor_{K/\Q}\kappa(Z)$ restricts to $\kappa(N_G(Z))$ on $K$, and the kernel of the restriction, $\operatorname{Hom}(G,\Z/8\Z)$, is detected by the Frobenius elements $\sigma_3$ and $\sigma_5$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:outside}
For every prime $p\notin S$ and every $P\in U(\Z_p)$, $\inv_p\mathcal A(P)=0$.

\begin{proof}
The primes of bad reduction of the model $f=0$ are $2$, $3$, $277$, $1321$ and $167149$: on the singular points, $f_y=0$ gives $x=-1-12y^2$, and the resultant with respect to $y$ of $f_x$ and $f$ after this substitution is $2^{41}\cdot3^{16}\cdot277\cdot1321\cdot167149$; the point at infinity is smooth over $\Z_p$ for odd $p$. \section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:proof}

Suppose that $(x,y)\in\Z^2$ satisfies $f(x,y)=0$, and let $P=(x,y)$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:mod7}, $(x,y)\bmod7$ is one of $(0,4),(0,5),(2,2),(4,6)$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:local8}, $8\inv_7\mathcal A(P)\in\{6,1,5\}$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:unram}, $\mathcal A(P)\in\Br(\Q)$ is defined, and $\sum_v\inv_v\mathcal A(P)=0$, the sum over all places of $\Q$. The real place contributes $0$, because $\mathcal A$ is a corestriction from the totally complex field $K$. (Alternatively, by Lemma~\ref{lem:nonzero8} all $H_i(P)$ are non-zero, and the relation $\sum_pb_p(P)=0$ follows directly from (H3) applied to \eqref{eq:bp}.) By Proposition~\ref{prop:outside} and Lemma~\ref{lem:local8},
\[
0=\sum_p8\inv_p\mathcal A(P)=d+2+s+4\in\Z/8\Z,\qquad d\in\{0,2,6\},\ s\in\{1,5,6\}.
\]
But $d+s+6$ takes the values $7,3,4$ for $d=0$, $1,5,6$ for $d=2$, and $5,1,2$ for $d=6$, never $0$. This contradiction proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. \qed

\section{Verification and formalization}\label{sec:lean}

\paragraph{Computations.} All identities, residue conditions and local values above were computed with exact arithmetic. The data of Section~\ref{sec:data} were then checked again by a separate program that reads them from the source of this file: the vertex identities and resultants of the cubic graph, the residue classes of Table~\ref{tab:local3} with Lemmas~\ref{lem:residue} and~\ref{lem:envelope} and the covering of $U(\Z_p)$, the $8$th power identities of Table~\ref{tab:roots8} on the Galois closure of the listed graph, the checks of Table~\ref{tab:descent8}, the elements \eqref{eq:Z} with the values at $307$ and $317$, the matrix $M_2$, and the data of Proposition~\ref{prop:outside}. An independent re-evaluation of $b_p$ at the $336$ odd sample points gave the values of Table~\ref{tab:local8odd}.

\paragraph{Lean.} The file \texttt{Solution.lean} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main} in Lean~4 with Mathlib; its proof uses only the axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound} and \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{comparator} checks that it proves exactly the statement of \texttt{Challenge.lean}. The formal proof follows the structure above, with two differences. It proves the reciprocity laws for the cubic and the $8$th power Hilbert symbols itself, within a development of local and global class field theory contained in the file. And it avoids purity and the Tate pairing: the vanishing outside $S$ is derived directly from the $8$th power identities of the graph on its Galois closure, separately at points reducing to a special point and at the other points, and at the primes of $S$ the values are computed on explicit finite covers of the integral points by residue classes ($5$, $53$, $25$, $21$, $9$, $1750$, $675$ and $582$ classes for $p=3,5,7,11,13,17,41,73$, and $512$ classes modulo $2^9$ at $2$). The file is large (about $58$ MB), and checking it takes about $12$ hours.
